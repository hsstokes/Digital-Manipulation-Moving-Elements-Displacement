What is This Workshop?

You will learn how to move and transform tape transfer elements in Photoshop for your fashion design projects, using tools like Content-Aware Move, Patch Tool, and Blend Modes.

Why learn digital manipulation?
  • Reposition elements without leaving gaps
  • Blend physical textures into digital layouts
  • Fix rough edges and clean up scanned material
  • Create professional, polished design sheets

Artist Inspiration

These contemporary artists use transfer techniques in their practice:

Robert Rauschenberg — Cot, 1980. Solvent transfer
Tavares Strachan — Four Hundred Meter Dash, 2018
Tauba Auerbach — Org, 2023
Important: Research and reference these artists properly if their work influences your design.

HOMEWORK: Make Your Tape Transfers

Do this before class. Bring your scanned files on the day.

📺 Watch This Video Before Class

Steps to Follow

1
Choose Your Materials

Collect a variety of tapes to experiment with:

  • Clear tape
  • Matte tape
  • Washi tape
  • Masking tape

💡 Tip

  • Only use laser prints — inkjet ink will not transfer
  • Try different printed materials: text, images, textures
2
Make 3–4 Transfer Samples
  • Press tape firmly onto your printed image
  • Rub well to transfer the ink
  • Peel back slowly and place onto white paper
  • Try different pressures and speeds when peeling
3
Scan Your Samples
  • Scan at 300 DPI or higher
  • Save as JPEG or TIFF
  • Make sure each sample is flat and clear
Important: Bring your digital files to class — save them to a USB or upload to your cloud storage.
4
Connect to Your Project

Think about how these transfers relate to your Term 2 project:

  • Material textures — does the tape echo fabric textures?
  • Layered design — how could transfers create depth?
  • Text elements — could transferred text tell your story?

TASK 1: Content-Aware Move & Fill

Learn to reposition elements and fill backgrounds automatically.

📺 Content-Aware Move Tool Tutorial

Content-Aware Move Tool

1
Select the Tool
  • Press J to select the Content-Aware Move tool
  • It shares a group with the Healing tools — click and hold to find it
2
Draw a Selection
  • Draw loosely around the element you want to move
  • Include a little space around the edges
3
Drag to New Position
  • Click and drag your selection to the new position
  • Release the mouse
4
Let Photoshop Blend

Photoshop automatically fills the gap and blends the moved element into the background.

Important: This works best on textured or patterned backgrounds. Solid colours may need extra clean-up.

📺 Content-Aware Fill Tutorial

Content-Aware Fill

1
Select the Area to Fill
  • Use the Magic Wand W or Lasso L to select the unwanted area
2
Open the Fill Dialog
  • Go to Edit → Content-Aware Fill
3
Adjust the Sampling Area
  • Paint green over areas you want Photoshop to sample from
  • Exclude areas that would look wrong in the fill
4
Apply

Click OK to apply. Deselect with Cmd/Ctrl + D.

TASK 2: Selection Tools for Fashion Elements

Use selection tools to isolate and move specific parts of your tape transfers.

Why use selection tools?
  • Cut out specific textures or shapes
  • Copy elements from one layer to another
  • Apply effects to only part of an image
Polygonal Lasso Tool

Best for selecting shapes with straight edges:

  1. Press L then toggle to Polygonal Lasso
  2. Click points around the object edge
  3. Connect back to start to close the selection
  4. Copy: Cmd/Ctrl + C
  5. Paste: Cmd/Ctrl + V
Magic Wand Tool

Best for selecting similar coloured areas:

  1. Press W
  2. Set Tolerance to 20–40 in the Options bar
  3. Click on the area you want to select
  4. Shift + Click to add to selection
  5. Alt/Option + Click to subtract
Remember to deselect when finished: Press Cmd/Ctrl + D to remove the "marching ants" selection outline before continuing.

💡 Tips for Fashion Design Work

  • Use Polygonal Lasso for cutting tape transfers into geometric shapes
  • Use Magic Wand to select white or light backgrounds quickly
  • Hold Shift to add multiple areas to your selection
  • Go to Select → Modify → Feather to soften selection edges

TASK 3: Patch Tool & Blend Modes

Fix rough edges and blend your tape transfers into your layout.

📺 Blend Modes Tutorial

Using the Patch Tool

1
Select the Patch Tool
  • Press J and toggle to the Patch Tool
  • Make sure Source is selected in the Options bar
2
Draw Around the Problem Area
  • Draw a selection around the rough edge or mark you want to fix
3
Drag to a Clean Source Area
  • Click and drag the selection to a clean area of the image to sample from
4
Release to Blend

Release the mouse and Photoshop blends the two areas together seamlessly.

Blend Modes

Change the Blend Mode of your tape transfer layer to integrate it with the background:

Recommended Blend Modes for Transfers:
  • Multiply — darkens, great for integrating transfers onto white or light backgrounds
  • Screen — lightens, useful for layering light textures
  • Overlay — increases contrast, makes textures pop
  • Soft Light — gentle contrast, good for subtle layering

💡 Dodge Tool for Highlights

  • Select the Dodge tool with O
  • Set Range to Midtones, Exposure to 20–30%
  • Paint over areas you want to lighten gently

TASK 4: Typography & Text Layout

Add text to your design sheet that supports — not competes with — your images.

Where to Use Text:
  • Titles — Page or section headings
  • Labels — Names for images and materials
  • Annotations — Short technical notes
  • References — Artist citations and sources

Recommended Fonts

Aim: Text should feel professional and balanced — it should support your images, not compete with them.

Recommended Options

Andale Mono — clean, technical look for fashion descriptions
Courier New / Orator Std — balanced spacing, good for annotations
Georgia / Grandview Display — modern, works well for headings
Typewriter Condensed — nostalgic, works with collage styles

💡 Typography Tips

  • Keep text size modest: 9–14pt is usually enough for A3 print layouts
  • Use consistent alignment — left-aligned or lightly centred text often works best
  • Avoid placing text directly over busy image areas — give it breathing room
  • Avoid overly decorative typefaces that fight with your imagery
  • Good typography tells the story quietly
Remember: Add Harvard references for any inspiration images you use in your work.
